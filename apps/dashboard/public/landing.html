How it works

Three ideas. One network.

  1. 01

    Anyone can offer or procure a capability.

    From a printer or centrifuge to a freelance service, a manufacturing process, or a complete business workflow. Each capability defines what it accepts, what it produces, and the conditions under which it can execute.

  2. 02

    Independently owned capabilities compose into complete production systems.

    Agents assemble independent operators into multi-step production systems. Those compositions can be reused and offered as capabilities of their own.

  3. 03

    Agentic coordination and continuous competition can make those systems cheaper to operate, more profitable to contribute to, and easier to participate in than a traditional company.

    Take the overhead of the vertically integrated firm out of the price and there is room for the customer to pay less and the operator to earn more, at the same time. Contributors keep ownership and control of what they bring, on the terms they agree to.

Why this beats the vertically integrated firm

Build production systems without owning the supply chain.

Vertical coordination without vertical consolidation of ownership

A traditional manufacturer bundles machines, people, management, procurement, scheduling, sales and logistics into one company, and most of what the customer pays goes to coordinating all of that rather than making the thing. PCC is building the market where agents do that coordination instead: they procure, contract, schedule and verify across independent owners. Take the overhead out and there is room for the customer to pay less and the operator to earn more, at the same time. The savings go to the people doing the work and the people buying it, not to a new middleman.

A company that owns its whole chain now has a structural problem: it is competing with a network of independent owners that charges customers less and pays producers more.

Hubs, not scattered factories.

Independent ownership is not dispersed factories. Its strongest form is a hub: independently owned CNC machines, robot arms, printers and metrology under one roof, sharing utilities, materials handling, logistics and safety, each owner competing to fulfil the network’s requests. The physical efficiency of vertical integration, without a single owner.

Contestable from the inside.

A vertically integrated company can have competitive suppliers while shielding its production decisions from the market. Here every step is a market. A better machine, process, controller or carrier displaces a worse one, and the system does not have to restructure for it to happen.

Inventions earn without a factory.

An engineer with a better machining process should not need to own a factory to earn from it. PCC is building toward designs, processes and software that are licensed into workflows and paid when they are used, on agreed terms, across every organization that runs them.

No gatekeeper. No platform tax.

Registration, discovery and interoperability stay cheap and open, and one task invoking another does not pay the network twice. The infrastructure exists so independent operators, developers, financiers and coordinators can build businesses on it, not so PCC can take a cut of every nested transaction.

Make it cheaper to produce, more profitable to contribute, easier to own, and impossible to capture value indefinitely without continuing to create it.
