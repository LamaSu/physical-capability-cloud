# Why — capability.network

*This is the content for the /why page on capability.network. Not features. Not docs. Just the belief.*

---

## The physical world has no API.

AI agents can trade stocks, deploy code, manage infrastructure, analyze data, and execute financial transactions — all programmatically, all autonomously.

Ask one to machine an aluminum bracket, and it's helpless.

Ask one to run a lab experiment, and it's helpless.

Ask one to translate a legal document with verified quality, and it's helpless.

The most capable AI systems on Earth terminate at the boundary of the physical world. Every agentic workflow hits the same wall: the moment it needs something *done* — physically made, physically moved, physically verified, or performed by a human with a specific skill — it stops.

---

## This isn't a technology problem. It's a protocol problem.

The machines are networked. The robots have APIs. The humans have skills. The agents have wallets. Stablecoins settle in under 500 milliseconds.

There is no protocol connecting them.

No universal format for expressing what a machine can do, what a person can do, what a robot can do. No standard for pricing that work programmatically. No cryptographic evidence system for proving it was done correctly. No composable settlement layer for paying for it automatically.

250,000 small manufacturers in the US alone run at 50-60% capacity utilization — machines idle, operators waiting — because there is no programmatic path between demand and capability. The $731 billion contract manufacturing market runs on email, phone calls, and PDF quotes that take 2 hours of manual labor each.

38 million MSMEs in India produce 30% of the country's GDP but capture only 1.8% of global exports. Not because they can't do the work. Because there's no protocol for discovery.

Hardware startups spend 3.5 months finding a contract manufacturer. Then wait 15.6 weeks for delivery. 97% fail to deliver on time. 67% experience quality failures that damage their finances. Not because manufacturing is hard — because *accessing* manufacturing is hard.

---

## What we're building.

A protocol — not a platform — that makes every capability on Earth discoverable, verifiable, and settleable by AI agents, humans, and machines.

**Capabilities, not machines.** You don't rent an EC2 server. You rent compute. You shouldn't need to know which CNC mill, which robot, which translator, or which lab instrument does the work. Describe what you need. The network matches.

**Evidence, not trust.** A job isn't done because someone said so. It's done when cryptographic evidence proves it — sensor logs, photos with perceptual hashes, dimensional measurements, process telemetry. Hash-chained, content-addressed, machine-verifiable. Your reputation follows you, not the platform you listed on.

**Protocol, not platform.** Xometry takes 35.7% of every transaction. Protolabs takes 44.6%. They own the relationship. They are the middlemen. TCP/IP didn't take a cut of internet commerce — it enabled $5 trillion of it. The value goes to the participants, not the protocol.

**Agents are the users.** 140 million agent payments were completed in the first 9 months of 2025. MCP standardized digital tool-calling across OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. The agent economy is here, transacting at scale. It has nowhere to route physical work. Until now.

---

## The scale of what's possible.

Today: 154 tools. Manufacturing capabilities — CNC, 3D printing, lab robots, inspection cameras. A live network with real escrow, real evidence, real robots executing real protocols.

Tomorrow: millions of tools. Every describable capability — human, machine, digital, physical, hybrid — expressed as a callable function in one universal protocol. A freelance translator in Seoul. A delivery drone in Nairobi. A CNC shop in Detroit. A lab robot in Boston. A graphic designer in Sao Paulo. All the same protocol. All composable. All verifiable. All settleable.

The global services economy is $70+ trillion. Zero percent of it runs on an open protocol for capability description, evidence, and settlement.

We're writing that protocol.
