# Every Capability. One Protocol.

## A Manifesto for the Physical Capability Cloud

---

There are two economies on Earth. One has an API. The other doesn't.

The digital economy — cloud compute, SaaS, financial services, media — became programmable decades ago. An AI agent can spin up a server, trade a stock, send a message, analyze a dataset, and deploy an application without a human touching anything. MCP gave agents 5,800+ digital tools in months. The tool catalog is exploding.

The physical economy — manufacturing, lab science, logistics, human expertise, robotics, inspection, maintenance, construction, agriculture — runs on phone calls, email chains, PDF quotes, handshake agreements, and 30-day invoices. A $731 billion contract manufacturing market where the average quote takes 2 hours of manual labor. A world where 97% of hardware startups fail to deliver on time, where machine shops run at 50-60% utilization while AI agents with wallets full of stablecoins cannot order a single machined part.

The physical world has no API.

We believe this is the most consequential gap in the modern economy, and we believe it's about to close.

---

### What We Believe

**1. Every capability can be described, priced, verified, and settled — whether the executor is carbon or silicon.**

A CNC mill that cuts aluminum at ±0.01mm. A human translator who handles Mandarin-to-English legal documents. A delivery robot with a 10kg payload in a 2km radius. A lab robot that runs HPLC analysis. A graphic designer who produces brand identity packages. A drone that surveys 50 acres at 2cm resolution. A solar panel installer who does residential rooftop. An AI agent that reviews code for security vulnerabilities.

All of these are capabilities. All of them can be expressed as structured descriptions with parameters, pricing rules, evidence requirements, and settlement conditions. The protocol doesn't care if the capability is delivered by a machine, a human, a robot, a piece of software, or some hybrid. It cares that the work was described, commissioned, verified, and paid for.

Not 154 tools. Not 5,800 tools. Millions. Every describable skill and capability on Earth becomes a callable function in a universal protocol.

**2. Capabilities, not machines. Not people. Not companies.**

You don't rent an EC2 *server*. You rent compute. You don't care which physical box in which data center runs your code. You care that it runs, that it meets the SLA, and that you pay per unit of work.

Manufacturing, services, and human expertise should work the same way. Describe what you need done — the material, the tolerance, the language pair, the delivery radius, the assurance level — and the network matches you to whoever can do it. Not which machine. Not which person. Not which company. Which *capability*.

This is how cloud compute won. It abstracted the hardware. We abstract the capability provider.

**3. Evidence, not trust.**

A job isn't done because someone said so. It's done when evidence proves it.

Cryptographic evidence. Photos with perceptual hashes. Sensor logs from the machine. Dimensional measurements. Process telemetry. Hash-chained, content-addressed, anchored on-chain. Not a PDF certificate emailed three weeks later. Not "we've worked with them before, they're reliable." Proof.

Every assurance tier defines what evidence is required. Tier 0 is self-reported. Tier 3 is third-party attested with ZK proofs and slashable bonds. The evidence is permanent, portable, and machine-verifiable. Your track record follows you, not the platform you happened to list on.

This doesn't just reduce disputes. It creates markets that can't exist without it. Automated quality evaluation. Trustless escrow that settles on data, not opinion. AI agents that can verify physical work without a human in the loop. An entire economy of evaluator nodes earning fees for attestation.

**4. Protocol, not platform.**

Every attempt to solve this problem before built a *platform*. A marketplace with a 30-45% take rate, a proprietary matching algorithm, a closed ecosystem, and a sales team. Xometry takes 35.7%. Protolabs takes 44.6%. They own the relationship between the buyer and the supplier. They are the middlemen with margin.

The physical world doesn't need another Uber. It needs TCP/IP.

A protocol that anyone can implement. That any agent can call. That any operator — from a machine shop in Detroit to a fabrication lab in Lagos to a freelance translator in Seoul — can register on and immediately access global demand. Zero gatekeepers. Zero relationship requirements. Zero minimum viable reputation. Your first job builds your first evidence bundle, and your evidence speaks for itself.

TCP/IP didn't capture 1% of internet commerce. It enabled $5 trillion of it. The value flowed to the participants, not the protocol. That's the design.

**5. Agents are the primary users.**

The dashboard exists. Humans can use it. But the primary interface to this network is not a web application — it's an API that AI agents call.

An agent discovers capabilities via DHT. Negotiates price via structured intents. Funds escrow with stablecoins. Monitors job progress via SSE streams. Receives evidence bundles. Triggers settlement. All programmatic. All autonomous. All composable.

140 million agent payments were completed in the first 9 months of 2025. MCP is the universal standard for digital tool-calling. The agent economy is here, it's transacting, and it has nowhere to route physical work.

We are writing the missing protocol.

### The Unspoken Compromise

Every category has a trade-off that everyone accepts but nobody likes. Here's ours:

**The physical world and the digital world operate on completely separate stacks, and everyone just... deals with it.**

AI agents can trade stocks, write code, manage cloud infrastructure, analyze data, send messages, and deploy applications. But ask one to machine an aluminum bracket, run an HPLC analysis, translate a legal document, deliver a package, or inspect a weld — and it's completely helpless. The most capable AI systems on Earth terminate at the boundary of the physical world.

250,000 small manufacturers in the US alone sit at 50-60% utilization, unable to access programmatic demand. 38 million MSMEs in India produce 30% of GDP but capture only 1.8% of global manufacturing exports — not because they can't do the work, but because there's no protocol for discovery. Hardware startups spend 3.5 months just finding a contract manufacturer, then wait 15.6 weeks for delivery, and 67% experience quality failures that damage their finances.

Everyone tolerates this. The $731 billion contract manufacturing market runs on email. The $70 trillion global services economy has no universal protocol for capability description, evidence, and settlement. 

We don't think this will be tolerated much longer.

---

### The Convergence Window

Three curves are crossing right now:

1. **AI agents are proliferating.** 79% enterprise adoption. MCP adopted by OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. 97 million monthly SDK downloads. Agents transact $4T+ in stablecoins in 7 months. They are ready to act — but only in the digital world.

2. **Physical capabilities are networkable.** IoT, industrial APIs, connected machines, camera-equipped robots, digital inspection systems. The hardware side is increasingly instrumented. It just lacks a coordination protocol.

3. **Cryptographic settlement is mature.** Stablecoins at $300B+ supply. Sub-500ms blockchain settlement. Programmable escrow. Zero marginal cost per transaction. The financial rails for automated, evidence-gated, milestone-based settlement exist and work.

The window is open. The agents have wallets. The machines have APIs. The settlement layer works. The only thing missing is the protocol that connects them.

That's what we're building.

### What This Becomes

Year one: manufacturing capabilities. CNC, 3D printing, lab instruments, inspection cameras. The machines we've already connected. The OT-2 robot in our lab running real protocols through real escrow on a live network.

Year two: every robot, every connected device, every automatable physical task. Delivery drones, agricultural sensors, construction equipment, cleaning robots, warehouse automation. Every piece of networked hardware becomes a callable capability.

Year three: human capabilities. Translation, design, consulting, inspection, training, tutoring, maintenance, installation. Every describable human skill expressed as a capability contract with evidence requirements and programmable settlement.

Year five: millions of capabilities. The long tail of everything any human, machine, or AI agent can do — described, discoverable, verifiable, and settleable through one protocol. The complete programmable interface to the physical economy.

Not a platform with a take rate. A protocol with a world.
